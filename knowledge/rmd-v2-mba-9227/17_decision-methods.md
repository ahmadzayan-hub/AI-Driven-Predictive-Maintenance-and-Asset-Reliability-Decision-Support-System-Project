## 17. Reusable decision-support methods
<!-- kb: domain=mba.9227.theory; limits=academic_case|not_rmd_operational_rule -->


The following are original synthesis and proposed working methods based on the user's requirements and the project lessons. They are not additional official course requirements or deployed RMD features.

### 17.1 Choose a solution proportionate to the decision

| Approach | Appropriate starting situation | Main question before adoption |
|---|---|---|
| Process improvement | Ownership, handovers or instructions cause the problem | Can a clearer process remove the problem without new technology? |
| Rules | The decision can be expressed with stable, approved conditions | Are the conditions complete, measurable and maintainable? |
| Automation | Repeatable work uses predictable inputs and actions | What happens on duplicates, missing data and partial failure? |
| Descriptive/diagnostic analytics | Managers need a reliable account of what happened and possible drivers | Are the data definitions and causal limits understood? |
| Predictive analytics | A useful future outcome can be labelled and predicted before action | Does prospective performance beat a credible baseline? |
| Optimisation/simulation | Managers must choose actions under constraints | Are objectives, resources and constraints correctly represented? |
| Generative AI | Drafting, summarisation or explanation can benefit from language processing | Can the output be grounded, reviewed and checked for unsupported claims? |
| Agent workflow | Several bounded steps need coordination across tools | Are permissions, state, failure recovery and accountability explicit? |

Do not recommend AI merely because the course or product includes AI in its name.

### 17.2 Business-case record

For each initiative, capture:

- Problem, accountable owner and decision.
- Baseline, measurable gap and affected stakeholders.
- Current process and alternatives, including a non-AI option.
- Data sources, quality, availability at decision time and applicable permissions.
- Build, buy, partner, hybrid and existing-enterprise options.
- Implementation cost, recurring cost, expected benefit and uncertainty.
- Integration, operational support, skills, adoption and change requirements.
- Safety, privacy, security, bias, explainability and reputational implications.
- Pilot population, comparison, KPIs, review gates and stop criteria.
- Assumptions, source references and unresolved evidence.

### 17.3 Quantitative interpretation rules

1. Preserve grain, numerator, denominator, time window, units and exclusions.
2. Keep observed values separate from targets, scenarios, forecasts and estimates.
3. Do not use association as evidence of causality.
4. Do not report commercial exposure as an achieved loss or saving.
5. Recalculate from traceable inputs where authorised; never silently invent missing inputs.
6. Compare a model against a simple baseline and the operational decision it enables.
7. Preserve uncertainty, subgroup performance and potential selection bias.
8. Avoid overclaiming statistical significance from a target being crossed.
9. Use deterministic calculation routines for official numbers and language generation to explain them.
10. Check whether implementation and review costs consume the proposed benefit.
