### 04.1 Original managerial learning notes

The following are concise learning and application notes, not quotations, full chapter summaries or an assertion that every topic is examinable. The chapter map above is source verified; the RMD applications below are proposed.

| Chapter area | Learning emphasis | Proposed RMD application | Question to challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Decision support | Start with the decision and responsible manager; organise intelligence, alternatives and choice | Define the management question before building a dashboard | What decision changes when this information appears? |
| 2. AI concepts | Match the technology to the task and data; distinguish rules, learning and language generation | Compare a rules-based review assistant with predictive or generative options | Does AI add value beyond a simpler process? |
| 3. Data and statistics | Understand grain, missingness, variation, denominators and sampling limitations | Reconcile operator reports and identify unreliable KPI inputs | Are these records comparable and complete enough? |
| 4. BI and visualisation | Build consistent definitions and a model that avoids double counting | Present verified maintenance KPIs with filters and source links | Does the visual support a decision or only decorate the page? |
| 5. Data mining | Define the target, prediction time, baseline and evaluation before model fitting | Design a failure-warning pilot with a chronological holdout | Can every feature actually be known at prediction time? |
| 6. Text analytics | Extract and organise text while checking ambiguity and source context | Identify recurring themes in maintenance reports and technical findings | Can the extracted statement be traced to its source? |
| 7. Deep learning | Assess data volume, task complexity, compute and the cost of error | Consider richer methods only when the task and evidence justify them | Is the gain useful enough to justify operational complexity? |
| 8. Optimisation and simulation | Define objectives, constraints and feasible alternatives | Compare resource or maintenance-window scenarios | Does the preferred action remain feasible under actual constraints? |
| 9. Tools | Evaluate tools against requirements, integration, cost, skills and ownership | Compare existing enterprise capability with new purchases | Are scores based on demonstrated capability or marketing claims? |
| 10. Trends | Separate durable ideas from changing product claims | Review agents, retrieval and automation through measured pilots | What is verified today and what is merely an announced capability? |
| 11. Ethics and management | Treat governance, adoption, privacy and accountability as design requirements | Bind evidence, human review and authorised actions to the exact case version | Who owns the decision, and what happens when the system is wrong? |

Use the textbook as the course's conceptual foundation. Current product capabilities, laws, regulations and technical guidance require current primary sources. Detailed RAG, agent orchestration, railway assurance and UAE-specific requirements must not be presented as fully covered or assessed merely because they are relevant to RMD.
