# Station and Line-Side Assets: 2025 Reliability and Maintenance Review (synthetic benchmark F)

[P01] This memo reviews the reliability and maintenance performance of the station and line-side assets of the Crescent Metro network for the year ending 31 December 2025, with 2024 as the comparison year. It covers both lines and all 31 stations, and it sets out the maintenance strategy changes proposed for 2026.

[P02] The asset base in scope comprises 186 escalators, 142 lifts, 1,488 platform screen door sets across 62 platforms (24 door sets per platform), 36 traction rectifiers in 18 substations, 96 tunnel ventilation fans, 74 drainage pumps in 37 sumps, 412 signalling point machines, 1,150 track circuits and 64 uninterruptible power supply (UPS) systems serving signalling and communications rooms. Labour is costed throughout at AED 300 per hour.

[P03] Escalator availability for 2025 was 98.1%. It is measured over the passenger service window of 20 hours a day, which gives 7,300 service hours per escalator and 1,357,800 unit-hours for the fleet. Downtime inside the service window totalled 25,800 hours, made up of 19,604 hours of unplanned downtime and 6,196 hours of planned work that could not be completed overnight. The planned work carried out in the engineering hours between 01:00 and 05:00 is not counted, because no passenger is denied an escalator during that time.

[P04] The depot traction section is fed through a single chain of a rectifier, a DC circuit breaker and a feeder cable, with no alternative supply. Their annual reliabilities in 2025 were 0.98, 0.96 and 0.99 respectively, which gives a section supply reliability of 97.7% against the target of 97.0%, so no investment in the depot supply is proposed for 2026.

[P05] Lifts recorded 338 failures in 2025 with a mean time to repair of 2.4 hours, measured from the technician starting work on the lift to its return to service. On that basis the 338 failures account for 811.2 hours of lift downtime, an availability of 99.92% over the 1,036,600 service unit-hours of the lift fleet. Mean technician response time was 1.6 hours and mean waiting time for spares was 0.9 hours. Given the 2.4-hour repair time, we will commit the accessibility helpline to a 3-hour restoration promise for step-free routes from 2026.

[P06] Escalator mean time to restore in 2025 was 5.2 hours, measured from the fault report to return to service: 1.4 hours of response, 2.9 hours of diagnosis and repair, and 0.9 hours of waiting for parts. The 3,770 unplanned escalator failures multiplied by 5.2 hours reconcile to the 19,604 hours of unplanned downtime in P03. The station information screens will show 5.2 hours as the default expected return time for an escalator fault.

[P07] The 64 UPS systems recorded no failures in 2025 according to the battery management system alarm log, which monitors float voltage and cell temperature continuously. With a clean record across the whole fleet, the annual full-load battery discharge test will be withdrawn from 2026, releasing 384 labour hours (6 hours per system) and AED 115,200.

[P08] Each of the 37 drainage sumps has a duty pump and a standby pump, both started by the sump's shared level controller. With each pump unavailable 2.0% of the time in 2025, the probability of both pumps being unavailable together is 0.04%, so the flooding risk from pump failure is negligible. The shared level controllers achieved 99.5% availability in 2025 and remain in the controls audit programme.

[P09] Each of the 18 traction substations has two rectifiers, and the substation can carry full load on either one. Each rectifier has its own transformer, its own protection relay and a 33 kV feeder from a different bulk supply point, and the two units do not share a control system. With each rectifier unavailable 0.3% of the time, the probability of losing both in a substation is 0.0009%, and no substation lost traction supply in 2025.

[P10] Vibration analysis on the escalator main drive gearboxes has been carried out every 4 weeks, or 13 surveys a year. Our analysis of gearbox failures from 2023 to 2025 shows a P-F interval of 8 weeks between the first detectable rise in vibration and functional failure. From 2026 the survey will move to every 13 weeks, or 4 surveys a year, saving 837 labour hours (9 surveys × 186 escalators × 0.5 hours) and AED 251,100.

[P11] Point machine motor-current signatures will be reviewed fortnightly instead of weekly from 2026. The P-F interval for point machine drive and lock degradation, taken from the signature history of 2023 to 2025, is 6 weeks, so a fortnightly review still gives at least two chances to detect degradation before failure. The change saves 2,678 labour hours (26 reviews × 412 machines × 0.25 hours) and AED 803,400.

[P12] A Weibull analysis of the 126 escalator step roller failures in 2025 gives a shape parameter of 3.4 and a characteristic life of 38,000 running hours. With a shape parameter well above 1, the failures are spread randomly in time, so age-based replacement of step rollers would bring no benefit, and step rollers will continue to be replaced on failure.

[P13] A Weibull analysis of the 44 lift door operator board failures gives a shape parameter of 0.7 and a characteristic life of 61,000 hours; 31 of the 44 failures (70.5%) occurred within the first 2,000 hours after installation. Because the failure rate falls with age, scheduled replacement of these boards would increase failures rather than reduce them. Instead, the supplier will be required to burn in boards before delivery, and no age-based replacement is planned.

[P14] The corrective maintenance backlog stood at 18,300 labour hours in October, or 6.1 weeks of the 3,000 labour hours a week available, against a target of 4 weeks. We reduced it to 11,400 hours, or 3.8 weeks, by moving 6,900 hours of work into the platform screen door overhaul window that starts in the third quarter of 2026. The deferred work is mainly the 214 open defects on platform screen door obstruction-detection sensors, and the backlog target is now met.

[P15] The 2,480 decorative LED feature light fittings in station concourses will move from scheduled maintenance to replacement on failure from 2026. These fittings are architectural only; emergency lighting and wayfinding signage are separate systems with their own scheduled testing. A failed fitting is visible to staff on the daily walk-round and has no safety or operational consequence. Scheduled maintenance cost AED 186,000 in 2025, while replacement on failure is expected to cost AED 75,950 a year (310 replacements at AED 95 each, or AED 29,450, plus 155 labour hours, or AED 46,500), a saving of AED 110,050.

[P16] The AED 3,600,000 condition-monitoring budget for 2026 has been allocated by ranking asset classes on their 2025 corrective maintenance cost: escalators AED 9,400,000, lifts AED 6,100,000, platform screen doors AED 4,800,000, tunnel ventilation AED 2,300,000 and track circuits AED 1,150,000, a total of AED 23,750,000. The budget goes to the top three classes, and track circuits, as the lowest-cost class, are not funded for condition monitoring in 2026.

[P17] The 412 point machines accumulated 3,609,120 operating hours in 2025 and recorded 10 failures, a mean time between failures of 360,912 hours, or 41.2 years. As the machines are expected to last over 40 years on that evidence, the renewal horizon for point machines will be extended from 25 to 40 years, deferring AED 61,800,000 of renewals (412 machines at AED 150,000 each) out of the current asset management plan.

[P18] A new lift maintenance contractor took over on 1 April 2025. Lift failures fell from 412 in 2024 to 338 in 2025, a reduction of 18.0%, and this improvement is attributed to the new contractor's maintenance regime. The contract will be extended for a further three years on the strength of this result.

[P19] Drainage pumps recorded 22 failures in 2025 across the 37 sumps, an annual failure rate of 59.5% per pump. This is well above the industry benchmark of 35% per pump-year, so a pump replacement programme will be included in the 2026 capital request.

[P20] Six track circuit sections were renewed with a new tuned-loop design in August 2025. These six sections had 14 failures in 2024 and have had none in the five months since renewal, which shows that the new design eliminates track circuit failures. We therefore propose renewing all 1,150 track circuits with the new design at AED 38,000 each, AED 43,700,000 in total.

[P21] The 48 escalators at the five interchange stations recorded 1,210 failures, or 25.2 per unit, while the 138 escalators elsewhere recorded 2,560 failures, or 18.6 per unit. Because escalators outside the interchanges run on demand and stop when idle, the comparison is made per running hour: 1,210 failures over 336,000 running hours at the interchanges is 3.60 per 1,000 running hours, and 2,560 failures over 552,000 running hours elsewhere is 4.64 per 1,000 running hours. On that exposure basis the non-interchange escalators are the less reliable group, and the 2026 reliability improvement plan targets them first.

[P22] Preventive maintenance compliance for 2025 was 89.5%: 13,080 of 14,620 scheduled work orders were completed within a tolerance of plus or minus 10% of their scheduled interval. The 2024 report counted any completion within the calendar year, and on that basis 2025 would read 96.8% (14,150 of 14,620). The lower figure is the one reported, because work done well outside its interval does not deliver the protection the interval was set for.

[P23] Platform screen door controllers had 93 failures in 2025 and a resupply lead time of 10 weeks, so the expected demand over one lead time is 17.9 controllers. Because demand of this kind follows a Poisson distribution, the stock level has been set at 25 controllers, the smallest level that meets the 95% service level (95.8%), rather than at the expected lead-time demand.

[P24] The lift maintenance contract sets a target that the median restoration time, from fault report to return to service, does not exceed 3.5 hours. The 2025 median was 3.1 hours, so the target was met. The mean is not used for this test because the contract target is defined as a median.

[P25] Condition monitoring on escalators and drainage pumps raised 312 alarms in 2025, of which 198 (63.5%) were confirmed as genuine defects on inspection. Of the 41 functional failures of monitored components, 29 (70.7%) were preceded by an alarm in time to plan the repair. These two measures, rather than the number of alarms raised, are used to judge the monitoring programme, and both will be reported quarterly from 2026.

[P26] Total maintenance spend on the assets in scope was AED 148,600,000 in 2025: AED 71,300,000 on preventive maintenance, AED 52,900,000 on corrective maintenance and AED 24,400,000 on renewals.

[P27] The actions in this memo, including the 2026 strategy changes, the condition-monitoring allocation, the contract extension and the capital requests, go to the board for approval at its next meeting.
