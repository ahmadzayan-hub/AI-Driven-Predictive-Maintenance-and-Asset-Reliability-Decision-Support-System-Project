# Regional Bus Depot Workshop Equipment: 2025 Reliability and Maintenance Review (synthetic benchmark K)

[P01] This review covers the workshop equipment at the operator's regional bus depot for the year ending 31 December 2025, with 2024 as the comparison year. The equipment in scope comprises 14 two-post vehicle lifts, 3 wheel lathes, 9 air compressors, 4 pit-mounted brake testers and 2 overhead cranes, giving 32 units in total. No units were added or retired during either year.

[P02] Each unit has 2,000 scheduled operating hours in a year, so the 32 units offer 64,000 scheduled hours in each of the two years. Scheduled hours are the exposure base for every rate in this memo, and hours when the depot is closed are outside the schedule.

[P03] Failure rates are expressed per unit or per 1,000 scheduled unit-hours, with the same exposure base applied to each equipment class in a comparison. Availability is one minus unplanned downtime divided by scheduled hours. Corrective cost includes parts and labour. Planned maintenance is reported in its own table and is not counted as downtime or as failure. Mean repair time is shown only with the number of events beside it.

[P04] Preventive maintenance performance improved over the period. Of the 1,200 preventive maintenance tasks scheduled in 2025, 1,128 were completed on time, which is 94.0%. In 2024 the depot scheduled 1,150 tasks and completed 1,001 on time, which is 87.0%. Both percentages use the number of scheduled tasks in their year as the base.

[P05] Wheel lathe failures fell from 3 in 2024 to 2 in 2025, a reduction of one-third (33.3%). The team regards this as a statistically significant improvement that confirms the new spindle bearing works, and recommends fitting the bearing to all three lathes on that basis. Two events in one year are the whole of the evidence, and the year's record describes what happened on these three lathes.

[P06] The four pit-mounted brake testers recorded 3 failures in 2025 across 8,000 scheduled hours (4 testers at 2,000 hours each). The failure rate is therefore 0.375 per 1,000 unit-hours. The repair cost of the three failures was 13,500 in total, or 4,500 per failure.

[P07] Compressors accounted for a material share of unplanned failures in 2025. The 9 air compressors recorded 5 failures, which is 56% of all unplanned workshop failures. Total unplanned failures across all equipment for the year were 17.

[P08] The failure mode study for the compressor receiver drain valve scores severity 7, occurrence 4 and detection 8, giving a risk priority number of 224 (7 x 4 x 8). Detection is scored on the workshop scale, where 10 means the failure is always detected before it causes harm. The risk priority number multiplies the three scores, so the valve's high detection score raises its priority, and the valve is placed first in the compressor register on that basis.

[P09] The lubrication interval trial allocated 8 of the 9 air compressors by lot draw. Four compressors moved to a 500-hour oil change interval and four remained on the existing 250-hour interval. Over 2025 the new-interval group recorded 0 failures and the existing-interval group recorded 3. The ninth compressor, outside the draw, recorded 2 failures. Because assignment was random, the trial shows that the new interval reduced failures among the compressors in the trial.

[P10] Oil analysis for compressor C-7 shows iron content of 30 ppm at the first sample, 60 ppm three months later and 90 ppm six months after the first. A straight line through these three readings rises by 10 ppm per month. The alert limit is 150 ppm, which the line reaches 6 months after the last sample, at month 12 from the first. The team therefore plans to overhaul C-7 at month 10 and takes month 12 as the expected failure date.

[P11] The brake testers recorded 3 failures in 2025, all of them in the first six months of the year. None of the four brake testers failed in the following six months, so the second half of the year recorded zero failures. Over those six months the four testers logged 4,000 scheduled hours between them.

[P12] Brake tester inspection records for 2025 list 7 findings in addition to the 3 functional failures. The team adds the findings to the failure numerator, giving 10 failures over 8,000 scheduled hours, or 1.25 per 1,000 unit-hours. That is more than three times the 0.375 that the functional failures alone give, and the team concludes that the brake testers need a redesign.

[P13] Across all equipment, unplanned failures fell from 19 in 2024 to 17 in 2025, and unplanned downtime fell from 392 hours to 351 hours. Fleet availability on the basis set out above rose from 99.39% to 99.45%. Corrective maintenance cost fell from 136,000 to 129,500, a reduction of 6,500 or 4.8%.

[P14] Lift failures were 6 in 2025 across 14 lifts, which is 0.43 failures per lift. In 2024 there were 9 failures across the same 14 lifts, which is 0.64 per lift. Both rates use the same unit count, so the two years are directly comparable.

[P15] For the year-end ranking, failure rates are placed on one list. Brake testers rank highest at 1.25 failures per 100 test cycles, from 3 failures in 240 cycles. Overhead cranes rank second at 0.50 failures per unit-year, from 1 failure across 2 cranes. The ranking therefore identifies the brake testers as the least reliable equipment class.

[P16] The lift seal trial fitted an upgraded hydraulic seal to 6 lifts and left 6 lifts on the standard seal, with the lifts paired by bay and workload. Over 2025 the 12 trial lifts recorded 5 leak-related failures: 1 on the upgraded seal and 4 on the standard seal. The remaining 2 lifts, outside the trial, recorded 1 failure. The paired design supports the statement that the upgraded seal reduced leak-related failures in this trial.

[P17] Lift failures fell from 9 in 2024 to 6 in 2025. The fall followed the change of grease supplier in March 2025, and the new grease caused the reduction in failures.

[P18] Wheel lathe 2 recorded no failures in 2025 over its 2,000 scheduled hours, while the three lathes together recorded 2 failures in the same year. The team sets the preventive maintenance interval for all three lathes from lathe 2's failure-free record, and proposes extending it from 6 months to 12 months on that basis.

[P19] The compressor planned overhauls covered all 9 air compressors, with each overhaul taking 8 hours, a total of 72 planned hours. These hours are excluded from unplanned downtime. Compressor unplanned downtime was 110 hours across 5 failures, giving a mean repair time of 22 hours per failure.

[P20] Replacing the overhead crane hoist costs 48,000 in capital. The 2025 crane repair cost was 18,000, so the simple payback is 2.67 years (48,000 divided by 18,000). On that payback, the committee should approve the replacement this year. The payback is calculated from the 2025 repair cost alone.

[P21] Wheel lathe failures recorded 60 hours of unplanned downtime across 2 failures in 2025, a mean of 30 hours per failure. With only 2 events the figure is reported to describe the year and is not used to set any interval.

[P22] Crew planning for compressor work uses a mean repair time of 22 hours per job as the typical job length. The five compressor repair durations were 2, 3, 4, 3 and 98 hours, a total of 110 hours, and their median is 3 hours. The 22-hour figure is the basis for the standard job allocation on the compressor roster.

[P23] The teardown of compressor C-3, which took 98 hours to repair in 2025, found a scored cylinder liner. The oil-level sensor had been faulty for 21 days before the failure, and the teardown report identifies the lubrication shortfall during that period as the cause of the scoring. The finding rests on the physical examination of the failed part.

[P24] Five of the 9 air compressors had at least one unplanned failure in 2025, and the 5 failures fell on 5 different compressors. That is 55.6% of the compressors, counted by unit.

[P25] Five lifts were taken out of service in 2025 for planned re-certification, each for 6 hours, a total of 30 hours. The team adds these 30 hours to the 96 hours of unplanned lift downtime, giving 126 hours. Across the 6 lift failures, the mean repair time is therefore 21 hours, which is the figure used to set the lift repair budget.

[P26] Twelve oil samples were taken from the air compressors in 2025. Every reading was below the 150 ppm alert limit for iron content. No sampled reading reached the alert limit during the year.

[P27] The compressor lubrication programme has removed the risk of compressor failure. The workshop recorded 5 compressor failures in 2025 and 4 in 2024, and the trial results allow the team to stop routine compressor monitoring in 2026.
