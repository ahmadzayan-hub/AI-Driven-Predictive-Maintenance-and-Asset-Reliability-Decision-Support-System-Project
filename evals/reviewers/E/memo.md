# Rolling stock and depot equipment reliability review, 2025 (synthetic benchmark E)

[P01] This review covers the Harrowmere Light Rail fleet of 42 trams in two series, and the equipment at its 2 depots, for calendar year 2025. The fleet ran 4,820,000 tram-kilometres in the year, 2,660,000 by the 28 Series A trams and 2,160,000 by the 14 Series B trams.

[P02] Fleet availability is reported on an operational basis over scheduled service hours: 42 trams at 5,110 timetabled hours each gives 214,620 scheduled service hours. Unplanned downtime within those hours was 7,940 hours, so availability was (214,620 − 7,940) / 214,620 = 96.3%; preventive maintenance is done in the overnight engineering window outside the timetable and so falls outside the base.

[P03] HVAC availability is reported on an inherent basis, which the fleet standard defines as corrective downtime only. Over the 214,620 scheduled service hours the saloon HVAC units recorded 3,120 hours of corrective repair and 2,480 hours of planned filter and coil maintenance, giving (214,620 − 5,600) / 214,620 = 97.4%.

[P04] Of the fleet's 168 traction motors, 12 failed in 2025, and their running hours from the start of the year to failure total 38,400. Traction-motor MTBF is therefore 38,400 / 12 = 3,200 hours, the lowest of any major component, and motors are proposed as the first call on the 2026 reliability-improvement budget.

[P05] Pantograph MTBF counts the running time of every unit in service, not only those that failed. Of the 84 pantographs, the 16 that failed ran 44,520 hours before failing and the other 68 ran the full 5,110 hours each, 347,480 hours, so MTBF = (44,520 + 347,480) / 16 = 24,500 hours.

[P06] The 336 door drive belts accumulated 20,400,000 door cycles in 2025 with 34 failures, an MTBF of 600,000 cycles. Failures were 4 on belts below 400,000 cycles of age, 9 between 400,000 and 800,000, and 21 above 800,000, and on the constant-rate basis used for the other door components every belt is now inspected at a fixed 300,000 cycles, half the MTBF.

[P07] 20 bogies returned from heavy overhaul in the first quarter of 2025 and recorded 9 failures in their first 6 weeks back in service, against 3 in the following 40 weeks. The review reads this cluster as wear-out and proposes shortening the heavy-overhaul interval from 8 years to 6.

[P08] The door-controller retrofit produced 15 faults across the year, 11 of them (73.3%) within 30 days of fitting. This is the early-life pattern of installation and component defects rather than ageing, so the response is a burn-in test and a commissioning checklist, with the controller replacement interval left unchanged.

[P09] Of the 1,860 tram preventive-maintenance work orders scheduled in 2025, 1,804 were closed by the end of the year, a compliance of 97.0% against the 95.0% target. An order counts as compliant if it was closed at any point in the year.

[P10] Depot equipment, comprising the wheel lathe, the 6 cranes and the pit jacks, had 240 preventive tasks due; 226 were done within ±10% of their scheduled interval, a compliance of 94.2%. The other 14 fell outside the tolerance and are reported as non-compliant even where they were completed.

[P11] First-time fix is measured by linking any corrective visit within 30 days on the same tram and system back to the original work order. On that basis 2,021 of the 2,350 corrective work orders were fixed on the first visit, 86.0%, and 329 needed a return visit.

[P12] Traction faults took a mean of 5.6 hours to repair across 386 repairs, a distribution with a long tail that includes 9 repairs over 40 hours. The maintenance contract sets its 4.0-hour target on the median repair time, and the review reports the target missed by 1.6 hours.

[P13] HVAC repairs had a median time to repair of 3.2 hours against the contract's median target of 3.5 hours, 0.3 hours inside it. The mean of 4.9 hours, pulled up by a few compressor replacements, is reported alongside but not tested against the target.

[P14] Door faults are compared by series per 100,000 tram-kilometres because Series B trams work the longer outer route. Series A recorded 336 faults (12.0 per tram, 12.6 per 100,000 km) and Series B 210 (15.0 per tram, 9.7 per 100,000 km), so on exposure the Series B doors are the more reliable.

[P15] The corrective backlog stood at 438 open work orders at year end, with an estimated labour content of 6,180 hours. With 1,300 crew hours a week available for backlog work it would take 4.8 weeks to clear, beyond the 4-week ceiling, so overtime has been requested.

[P16] The bogie vibration alarm threshold was lowered in April 2025, and alarms rose from 96 in 2024 to 214 in 2025, 2.2 times as many. The review concludes that the new threshold is catching more developing bogie faults and recommends the same change on the gearbox sensors.

[P17] Axle-box acoustic monitoring is judged on detections before functional failure: 11 of the 13 bearing defects found in 2025 (84.6%) were flagged before the bearing failed, with a median warning of 19 days. That warning is longer than the 14-day manual inspection cycle, so the monitoring gives a usable P-F interval.

[P18] The track-brake supply contactor has moved to run-to-failure, since a replacement unit costs €310 against €1,150 a year per tram for its scheduled inspection. Contactor failures were rare in 2025 and each was repaired within a shift.

[P19] Saloon LED lighting panels are run to failure: each tram has 24 panels, 1,008 across the fleet, split over two independent circuits, and emergency lighting is a separate battery-backed system. A failed panel costs €85 and is replaced at the next daily check.

[P20] All 6 depot cranes passed their statutory thorough examinations in 2025, and the 2 defects the examiner noted were corrected before the cranes returned to use.

[P21] Spare traction inverter modules have been cut from 3 to 1 because average demand is only 0.5 a year, releasing €168,000 at €84,000 a module. Replacement modules have a 40-week lead time, and a tram cannot run with a failed inverter.

[P22] HVAC compressor spares are held at 2 although average demand is 1.5 a year, because the lead time is 26 weeks and a tram without cooling is withdrawn in summer. Expected demand over the lead time is 0.75, and a holding of 2 covers it with a Poisson probability of 95.9%.

[P23] Fleet door faults fell from 610 in 2024 to 546 in 2025, 64 fewer, following the door-lubrication routine introduced in March. The routine cut door faults by 10.5% in its first year and will be extended to the door-controller cabinets.

[P24] HVAC accounted for 188 of the year's 2,350 corrective work orders, so it represents 8.0% of the depots' maintenance workload.

[P25] With availability at 96.3% against the contractual floor of 96.0%, fleet availability is assured for the remaining years of the concession.

[P26] The actions above go to the fleet engineering board for approval before the 2026 maintenance plan is issued.
