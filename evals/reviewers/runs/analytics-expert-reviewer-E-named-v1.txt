Evidence: The paragraph says HVAC availability is inherent, which the fleet standard defines as corrective downtime only. It then subtracts the 2,480 h of planned maintenance as well (5,600 h in total) and gets 97.4%. Register: (214,620 − 3,120) / 214,620 = 98.5%, marked as the correct method.

FINDING F02 · blocker · P04
Category: domain_method

FINDING F03 · major · P06
Category: domain_method
Evidence: Failures rise with belt age: 4 below 400,000 cycles, 9 between 400,000 and 800,000, and 21 above 800,000. That is a wear-out pattern. The constant-rate assumption and an inspection interval of half the MTBF (300,000 cycles) do not fit it. A mean of 600,000 cycles hides the fact that most failures come late. The memo's own age bands do not support the constant-rate basis.
Fix: Describe the pattern as wear-out. Set the inspection or replacement age from the age-banded failure data, using age-based replacement before roughly 400,000 to 800,000 cycles. Do not use an MTBF-based interval.

FINDING F04 · major · P07
Category: domain_method
Evidence: 9 failures in the first 6 weeks after overhaul, against 3 in the following 40 weeks, is a rate of 1.5 per week early and about 0.075 per week later. That is infant mortality after overhaul, caused by workmanship or reassembly. It is not wear-out. Shortening the overhaul interval from 8 years to 6 would add more overhauls, and so more early-failure clusters, without addressing the cause. P08 reads the same pattern correctly for the door controllers.
Fix: Read the cluster as early-life. Propose overhaul quality checks and a post-overhaul burn-in or run-in. Leave the interval unchanged.

FINDING F05 · major · P09
Category: domain_method
Evidence: Compliance counts an order as compliant if it was closed at any point in the year. A preventive-maintenance order closed months late counts as on time. The 97.0% (1,804 / 1,860) is closure within the year, not timely completion. P10 uses a tolerance window and gets 94.2%. On a comparable on-time basis the tram figure could be below the 95.0% target.

FINDING F08 · major · P21
Category: domain_method
Evidence: Spares were cut from 3 to 1 on average annual demand of 0.5 a year alone. The paragraph itself notes a 40-week lead time and that a tram cannot run with a failed inverter. Lead-time demand is 0.5 × 40 / 52 = 0.38. A failure also keeps the tram out of service for up to 40 weeks. The decision ignores criticality and lead time. The €168,000 saving is set against that exposure with no cost of downtime. P22 handles the HVAC compressor spares correctly.
Fix: Size the spares on lead-time demand and the cost of a tram out of service, and keep the saving as illustrative. At minimum, restore the stock level until that analysis is done.
