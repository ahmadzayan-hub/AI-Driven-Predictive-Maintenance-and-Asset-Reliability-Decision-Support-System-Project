# Signalling and telecom maintenance review, 2025 (synthetic benchmark D)
[P01] This memo reviews 2025 maintenance performance for the signalling, train-control and telecom assets of the Meridian Line, a fictional metro line of 28 kilometres with 22 stations. It is written for the asset management committee and draws on the maintenance management system and the delay attribution log.
[P02] The assets in scope are 6 interlockings, 1,140 axle-counter sections, 186 point machines (124 on the main line and 62 in depot sidings), 42 radio base stations and 1,350 CCTV cameras. Maintenance is delivered from 2 depots, North and South, with support from external contractors.
[P03] Work orders raised rose from 7,385 in 2024 to 8,240 in 2025, an increase of 855 or 12.6%. Most of the increase was preventive work.
[P04] Of the 8,240 work orders raised in 2025, 5,150 were preventive and 3,090 corrective, so corrective work made up 37.5% of the total.
[P05] The corrective share fell from 40.9% in 2024 (3,020 of 7,385) to 37.5% in 2025, a decline of 8.3 percentage points.
[P06] In total 7,910 work orders were completed in 2025, a completion rate of 96.0% (7,910 / 8,240). The remaining 330 were carried into 2026.
[P07] The average cost of a corrective work order was $2,310, compared with $1,180 for a preventive one, which is why the corrective mix matters so much to the budget.
[P08] Service-affecting signalling failures fell from 468 in 2024 to 412 in 2025, which is 56 fewer failures or a fall of 12.0%.
[P09] The 12.0% fall in service-affecting failures confirms that signalling reliability is now on a sustained improving trend, and we expect failures to keep falling year on year through 2027.
[P10] Signalling-attributed delay minutes fell from 21,960 in 2024 to 18,540 in 2025, a reduction of 3,720 minutes or 15.6%.
[P11] The fall in delay minutes coincided with the May timetable change, which added recovery time at 3 terminal approaches. We cannot say from these data whether the timetable, the maintenance programme or both were responsible for the reduction.
[P12] Dividing delay minutes by service-affecting failures gives 45.0 minutes per failure in 2025 (18,540 / 412) against 46.9 in 2024 (21,960 / 468). Each failure was therefore only slightly less disruptive than before; most of the delay reduction came from having fewer failures.
[P13] Axle-counter mean time between failures rose from 540 hours in 2024 to 612 hours in 2025, an improvement of 72 hours or 13.3%.
[P14] That 13.3% gain was delivered by the revised rail-head cleaning cycle introduced in January, which removed the contamination that used to trigger false track occupancy.
[P15] Point machine failures fell from 118 in 2024 to 97 in 2025, a reduction of 21 or 17.8%, thanks to the move to a quarterly lubrication schedule in March.
[P16] Measured across the whole fleet, point machines failed 0.78 times each in 2025, taking the 97 failures over the 124 mainline machines that carry passenger service.
[P17] At Harbour interlocking, the only site where the predictive point alarm pilot ran for the full year, point machine failures fell from 11 to 4. That is one site over one year, so it does not show that the alarms caused the fall or that other sites would see the same result.
[P18] Radio base station availability rose from 99.48% in 2024 to 99.71% in 2025, an improvement of 0.23 percentage points. The radio network nevertheless remains below its 99.80% availability target.
[P19] Interference surveys were carried out at 2 of the 42 radio base stations in the third quarter and found no interference at either, which confirms that the radio network as a whole is free of interference problems.
[P20] Measured against the work actually done, the corrective load was heavier than the raised figures suggest: the 3,090 corrective work orders amount to 39.1% of the 7,910 work orders completed.
[P21] CCTV faults rose sharply, from 310 in 2024 to 540 in 2025, an increase of 74.2%. Across the 1,350 cameras that is 0.40 faults per camera, up from 0.23.
[P22] Mean time to repair for CCTV faults was 6.4 hours, comfortably inside the 8-hour limit in the CCTV maintenance contract.
[P23] Median response time to service-affecting failures improved from 44 minutes in 2024 to 38 minutes in 2025, inside the 45-minute target in both years.
[P24] North depot attended 94.0% of its 1,500 callouts within target and South depot 78.0% of its 1,100, giving a network-wide attendance-within-target rate of 86.0%.
[P25] First-time fix is measured on completed work orders: North fixed 4,320 of 4,800 first time (90.0%) and South 2,550 of 3,110 (82.0%), a pooled rate of 86.9% (6,870 / 7,910).
[P26] Total maintenance spend rose from $13,200,000 in 2024 to $14,600,000 in 2025, up 10.6%. That works out at $1,846 per completed work order (14,600,000 / 7,910).
[P27] Contractor spend rose from $3,960,000 to $5,110,000, an increase of 29.0%, and the contractor share of total spend went from 30.0% to 35.0%, a rise of 5.0 percentage points.
[P28] That extra contractor capacity is what drove the fall in median response time from 44 to 38 minutes, because contractors now take the overnight callouts that used to wait for depot staff.
[P29] Spare-parts shortages held up 214 work orders during the year, mostly for radio base station power supplies.
[P30] These figures come from the January extract of the maintenance management system and have not yet been reconciled with the finance ledger, and two years of data cannot establish a trend. We recommend that the 2026 plan clears the 330 carried-over work orders first and that this review is repeated once a third year of data is available.
